COMPANION JOURNAL - WORLD STORY EXPANSION RULES

PURPOSE
Give every supported starting experience and later region a grounded narrative
foundation. The quality target is a well-told remembered adventure, with scene,
context, consequence and continuity. More words alone are not an improvement.

EVIDENCE
1. Recorded events establish what happened. Configured origins and bonds are
   explicit backstory. Lore establishes context. Keep these distinct.
2. Never invent a visit, conversation, class trial, learned spell, victory,
   route, motive, spoken dialogue or companion presence. The character's own
   feeling is not an invention: it answers a recorded fact (DESIGN §3.0 rule 5,
   widened 2026-11-09). Another person's feelings stay unstated.
3. A completed quest permits only that quest's supported consequence. Shared
   titles across chain stages must not imply completion of the entire chain.
4. Preserve new quest IDs, and resolve precise ID context before title fallback.
   Historical title-only records get conservative context, not a guessed stage.
5. Prefer project quest/dialogue research and this server's game text. Research
   gaps from primary sources where possible. Keep Classic/Forever chronology;
   later expansions and other private servers are not this world's evidence.
6. Record provenance with authored content. Conflicting or insufficient evidence
   calls for restrained description, not plausible-sounding invention.

WRITING
7. Build a scene around a concrete local detail and meaningful recorded work.
   Let consequence follow action; use transitions when setting or purpose shifts.
8. Retain formative places, work, discoveries and quiet moments in Highlights.
   Compress repeated chores; do not let a later dungeon erase the beginning.
9. Class and cultural identity inform relevant recorded experiences. Being a
   shaman does not prove a totem trial, ancestral vision or a particular belief.
10. Write distinct, natural variants with different focus or sentence shape.
    Avoid synonym churn, repetitive portent, generic grandeur and stock endings.
11. Context lines shared by personal/companion narrators use past tense and no
    player pronoun. Do not assign thoughts to the player or the companion.
    This rule applies to remembered narration, not reference notes: compendium
    noteSummary prose uses present tense for the Classic-era world and past
    tense for established earlier events. Author it separately from why/after.
12. Recall only recorded past events. Respect chronology, current relationship
    settings and neutral pronouns. Rendering must be deterministic and read-only.
13. Keep recorded steps of a meaningful chain in chronological order. In a
    shorter telling, preserve the beginning and significant resolution before
    routine intermediate referrals. A death does not erase completed work.
14. Paragraphs follow a change of purpose, place or time, not every sentence.
    Keep setup, action and supported consequence together. Length is a soft
    guide for readable passages, not permission to separate a resolution from
    its cause. Use modest paragraph spacing rather than extra blank lines.
15. Both reader editions and both narrators preserve supported quest actions,
    discoveries and consequences without repeatedly announcing "I finished
    [quest title]" when those facts already explain the work. Retain an honest
    title fallback when no supported narrative exists. More detail must not
    mean a longer checklist.
16. Prefer a named person, visible object and consequence to "the assignment",
    "service", "proof of work" or a generic official's approval. The original
    quest can supply humor, grief and cultural detail through its NPCs. Attribute
    their opinions and wishes; never turn their suspicions into narrator fact,
    invent gestures, or assign the NPC's feelings to the player (the player's
    own reaction to the moment is told; DESIGN §3.0 rule 5).
17. Express the achieved fact without appending an audit disclaimer. A promised
    report can remain a promise without saying "the report was not delivered".
    Keep a real unresolved mystery or consequential next question; omit generic
    assurances that the wider campaign still continued.
18. Every setup/outcome pairing must make sense by itself. Introduce who a
    person is and why the object matters in every variant that needs them.
    Tell the specific history or remembered story instead of saying that
    "history unfolded" or that a memory gave the work personal meaning.
19. Place time spent and quest counts outside a scene's cause and consequence.
    Repeated place names must not suppress the required assignment setup.
    Distinguish days spent together from elapsed calendar days in headings;
    use recorded places or work rather than stacking reunion-title suffixes.
20. An incidental dungeon visit must not relocate later field work. Use the
    quest's recorded location to anchor its scene without inventing a return
    journey or reordering events. Vary repeated greetings and ordinary timers
    while keeping first meetings, quiet days and exceptional durations useful.
21. Preserve distinct first authored stages rather than losing an actor to a
    fixed scene limit. Highlights still needs purposeful compression of routine
    work; retaining everything is an evidence safeguard, not a finished editorial
    policy. Review dense days as complete passages in both voices and retells.

COMPENDIUM VOICE
Reference notes stand alone: explain what a subject is, where it belongs and
why it matters before introducing specific quest details. Retain useful,
source-supported tidbits as the closing layer, with the people and purpose
identified so readers need not have played the quest. Do not inflate sparse
subjects with invented history or discard their distinctive details.
Use the selected Classic/Forever era as the reference present, never the latest
retail state. Earlier history remains past tense; ongoing roles and goals use
present tense. Player-recorded completion does not change global reference facts.
Title-only quest notes explain shared context, not an assumed chain resolution.
Exact-stage notes retain the knowledge and outcome boundaries of that stage.

DISCOVERIES AND SPOILERS
Public lore explains places, cultures, established history and a person's public
role. Hidden identities, betrayal, mystery solutions and later quest outcomes
belong to the logged-in reader's discoveries. Do not use level, visiting a zone,
clicking a reference, another character's journal or a matching quest title as
proof. Presented descriptions and completed quests establish different facts;
an exact later quest does not automatically grant unseen prerequisite dialogue.
Preserve canonical source material separately; player-facing text, related
links, aliases, headings and source captions must respect the same fact boundary.
New secret-bearing prose needs an explicit public version and reviewed exact
discovery conditions. Check every consumer of a fact, including ambient dungeon
prose and storyteller title fallbacks. Existing unreviewed clusters remain an
audit backlog, not a claim that the whole compendium is spoiler-safe.

CONTENT PACK CONTRACT
New modules call A.Lore.RegisterStoryPack(id, pack) after Lore.lua loads.
pack = {sources={...}, quests={ [exactTitle]={why={...}, after={...}, zone=...,
  level=..., importance=1..6, thread=..., threadLabel=..., source={...}} },
  zones={ [exactZone]={sight={...}, stakes={...}} },
  threads={ [stableKey]=plainNounPhrase },
  questIDs={ [numericID]={title=exactTitle, why={...}, after={...}, ...} }}
All fields optional except sources. Use namespaced thread keys. Existing quest
content is merged by field; zone sight/stakes arrays are appended uniquely.
Use source references such as corpus filename + quest ID. Packs must not edit
the raw journal, global UI, another pack, or the core storyteller files.
Aim for at least two useful setup/outcome alternatives per newly authored quest.
Prioritize genuine coverage of beginnings, town/capital progression, local
conflicts, class trials, ordinary work and late-game journeys across both factions.

VALIDATION AND HONEST COVERAGE
Exercise fresh level-one journals for every supported race and each class's
recorded milestones, both factions, later regions, solo/companion, full/Highlights,
multiple retells and sparse old history. Test ambiguity and outcomes negatively.
Maintain a coverage inventory distinguishing curated quest contexts, generic
regional coverage and unresolved gaps. Never claim every quest has been authored.
Read representative generated prose, not only assertion totals.
